    Method & sources

    What the model does

    Slicebench starts at incorporation with 10,000,000 fully diluted shares. The option pool is reserved as unissued shares. Founder weights split the remaining issued common.

    Post-money SAFEs

    A post-money SAFE with purchase amount A and cap C is promised A / C of the company immediately after conversion and immediately before new priced-round money or a new pool refresh. That follows the idea behind the standard Y Combinator post-money SAFE (2018 onward). Multiple post-money SAFEs stack their promised percentages.

    Pre-money SAFEs

    A pre-money SAFE converts as shares = amount × pre-SAFE fully diluted shares / cap, issued simultaneously so pre-money SAFEs do not dilute each other.

    Discount versus cap

    If a SAFE also has a discount and a priced round is on the bench, conversion uses the lower of the cap price and the discounted round price. The engine iterates once so the discount can be applied against the round's price per share.

    The option-pool shuffle

    When a priced round sets a post-money option-pool target, new unissued pool shares are created on a pre-money basis so that the unissued pool equals that target of post-money fully diluted shares. New cash investors are not diluted by the refresh. This is the common "option-pool shuffle" in seed term sheets.

    Vesting

    Employee grants vest monthly over the vest period, with a cliff. Leave before the cliff and nothing has vested. Unvested shares are treated as forfeited for the paper-value math.

    What we do not model

    Liquidation preferences, pro rata, MFN, unpaid accrued interest on notes, IRS 83(b) elections, AMT, QSBS, 409A strike prices, and secondary sales. Paper value is ownership × assumed exit, with no taxes and no preference stack. Use it to compare shapes, not to bank a number.
